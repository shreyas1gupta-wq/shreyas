# Bibliography

Consolidated references for the cycle stack. Entries marked **[verify]** are ones where I
am confident of the finding but not of the exact title, year or venue — check before citing
externally. Entries marked **[free]** are openly accessible, which matters given the
free-data constraint.

---

## 1. Cycle theory — classical

- Kitchin, J. (1923). "Cycles and Trends in Economic Factors." *Review of Economic
  Statistics*, 5(1). — The 3–5 year inventory cycle.
- Juglar, C. (1862). *Des Crises commerciales et leur retour périodique en France, en
  Angleterre et aux États-Unis.* — The 7–11 year fixed-investment cycle.
- Kuznets, S. (1930). *Secular Movements in Production and Prices.* — 15–25 year
  infrastructure swings.
- Kondratiev, N. (1925). "The Major Economic Cycles." Translated as "The Long Waves in
  Economic Life," *Review of Economic Statistics*, 17(6), 1935.
- Schumpeter, J. (1939). *Business Cycles: A Theoretical, Historical and Statistical
  Analysis of the Capitalist Process.* — The three-cycle schema.
- Burns, A. & Mitchell, W. (1946). *Measuring Business Cycles.* NBER. **[free]** — The
  foundational reference-cycle dating methodology.
- Solomou, S. (1998). *Economic Cycles: Long Cycles and Business Cycles Since 1870.* —
  The careful skeptical treatment of Kondratiev. Read this before believing in long waves.
- Hoyt, H. (1933). *One Hundred Years of Land Values in Chicago.* — The land/property cycle.

## 2. Financial cycle and credit — the strongest evidence base

- Borio, C. (2014). "The financial cycle and macroeconomics: What have we learnt?"
  *Journal of Banking & Finance*, 45. **[free — BIS working paper version]**
- Drehmann, M., Borio, C. & Tsatsaronis, K. (2012). "Characterising the financial cycle:
  don't lose sight of the medium term!" *BIS Working Paper No. 380.* **[free]**
- Schularick, M. & Taylor, A. (2012). "Credit Booms Gone Bust: Monetary Policy, Leverage
  Cycles, and Financial Crises, 1870–2008." *American Economic Review*, 102(2). — The
  single most robust quantitative result in the cycle literature.
- Jordà, Ò., Schularick, M. & Taylor, A. (2013). "When Credit Bites Back." *Journal of
  Money, Credit and Banking*, 45(s2).
- Jordà, Ò., Schularick, M. & Taylor, A. (2017). "Macrofinancial History and the New
  Business Cycle Facts." *NBER Macroeconomics Annual*, 31.
- **Jordà–Schularick–Taylor Macrohistory Database** — 18 advanced economies, 1870–present.
  **[free]** `macrohistory.net/database` — *the* enabling dataset for cross-country pooling.
- Aikman, D., Haldane, A. & Nelson, B. (2015). "Curbing the Credit Cycle." *Economic
  Journal*, 125(585).
- Reinhart, C. & Rogoff, K. (2009). *This Time Is Different: Eight Centuries of Financial
  Folly.* — Crisis clustering and sequencing.
- **BIS credit-to-GDP gap statistics**, including India. **[free]** `bis.org/statistics`

## 11. India-specific

Thinner and less certain than the above. Verify before relying on any of it.

- **Agarwalla, S., Jacob, J. & Varma, J. R. — the IIM Ahmedabad Indian factor library.**
  **[free]** `faculty.iima.ac.in/~iffm/Indian-Fama-French-Momentum/` — Fama-French and
  momentum factor returns for India, maintained and downloadable. **This is the single most
  valuable free India-specific quantitative resource available and should be the benchmark
  for every factor we construct.** Associated paper: "Four factor model in Indian equities
  market," IIMA Working Paper W.P. No. 2013-09-05. **[verify exact citation]**
- Reserve Bank of India — *Database on the Indian Economy (DBIE)*, `data.rbi.org.in`.
  **[free]** — The primary macro source: rates, credit, money, external sector, house
  prices, OBICUS capacity utilisation.
- RBI — *Financial Stability Report* (semi-annual). **[free]** — Stress tests, NPA
  projections, credit-cycle commentary. Underused as a systematic data source.
- MOSPI — national accounts, CPI, IIP. **[free]**
- NSE and BSE — bhavcopy archives (NSE from 1994, BSE from 2007), index constituent
  circulars, shareholding filings, F&O participant-category data. **[free]**
- CCIL — GSec yield curve and traded data. **[free]**
- World Gold Council — gold demand trends and central-bank purchases. **[free]**
- IMF COFER — currency composition of FX reserves. **[free]**

## 12. Reading order

If only five things are read before building starts:

1. Hamilton (2018) — because it changes how every cycle in the model is extracted.
2. Goyal & Welch (2008) — because it sets the honest prior on return predictability.
3. Harvey, Liu & Zhu (2016) — because it sets the evidentiary bar.
4. Daniel & Moskowitz (2016) — because momentum crashes are the specific mechanism most
   likely to breach the drawdown constraint.
5. Borio (2014) + Schularick & Taylor (2012) — because the credit cycle is the anchor of
   the whole stack and has the best evidence in it.
